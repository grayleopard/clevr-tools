You need a PDF page as an image—maybe for a presentation, a social media post, an email where you cannot attach a PDF, or a website that only accepts image uploads. Here is how to export the pages as JPG images.

## How to convert PDF to JPG

1. Open the [PDF to JPG Converter](/convert/pdf-to-jpg)
2. Drop your PDF into the upload area (or click to browse)
3. Choose your quality setting — higher quality means larger files
4. Select which pages to convert (all pages, or specific pages)
5. Download your JPG images

Each PDF page becomes a separate JPG image. The entire conversion runs in your browser — your PDF never leaves your device.

## Choosing quality settings

The quality slider controls the JPG compression level of the output images:

- **Higher quality**—preserves more output detail but creates larger files. Start here for text, presentations, or print-oriented workflows.
- **Medium quality**—can balance size and detail for many screen uses. Inspect small text and diagrams before sharing.
- **Lower quality**—creates smaller files but can soften text or introduce visible artifacts. Reserve it for cases where the destination is small or has a strict limit.

For documents with text, begin with a high setting and inspect the exported letters at the intended size. Blurry edges and halos are signs that the setting is too low.

Photo-heavy PDFs may tolerate more compression than text-heavy documents, but gradients, fine patterns, and faces still need a visual check.

## Single page vs full document

Most of the time, you don't need every page as a JPG. Common use cases:

- **First page only** — for a document preview or thumbnail. Convert just page 1.
- **Specific pages** — extracting charts, graphs, or images from a report. Select only the pages that contain what you need.
- **Full document** — when you need every page as an image (e.g., converting a slide deck PDF for use in a website gallery or email sequence).

## Why not just screenshot the PDF?

Screenshots capture the pixels currently shown on your screen and can include scaling artifacts. A converter renders the PDF page directly to a clean image, without browser chrome or unrelated screen content.

Screenshots also include your UI — browser chrome, scroll bars, other tabs. The converter produces clean, full-page images with nothing extra.

### Does converting PDF to JPG reduce quality?

Some quality loss is inherent in JPG compression. For text-heavy documents, start with a high setting. For photos and graphics, compare a few settings at the intended display size and keep the smallest acceptable result.

### Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

If your PDF requires a password to open, you'll need to unlock it first. If it's only restricted from printing/copying (but opens without a password), the converter should handle it.

### What about PDF to PNG instead of JPG?

PNG is lossless — no compression artifacts, ever. Use PNG when you need pixel-perfect output (screenshots, diagrams with text, technical documents). Use JPG when file size matters more than perfection (photos, web images, email attachments).

### How many pages can I convert at once?

Processing happens in your browser, so the practical page limit depends on document complexity and available memory. For large documents, convert in batches or extract only the pages you need.
